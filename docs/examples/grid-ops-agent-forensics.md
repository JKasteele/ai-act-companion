Can the organisation reconstruct and evidence what this system did, why, with which data and model version — after an incident, a complaint, a regulator request or a dispute? The EU AI Act makes logging a design requirement ([Art. 12](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/12/)), retention a duty ([Art. 19](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/19/), [Art. 26](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/26/)(6)) and evidence preservation an implicit duty ([Art. 73](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/73/): do not alter the system before reporting). Model output is non-deterministic, so **evidence comes from recording, not from re-running**. This plan says which evidence should exist and where; it is not a SIEM.

## 3. Integrity, time and chain of custody
- Log integrity: hash_chain_worm — target: tamper-evident records (hash chain + WORM, or signed with an independent time anchor), stored apart from the monitored system, with access to the logs itself logged (ISO 27001 5.28, 8.15; CIS Control 8.9; NIS2 guidance §3.2.5).
- Time synchronisation: Yes — one time source across application, gateway, workflow and data-access logs (ISO 27001 8.17; CIS 8.4).
- Chain of custody: who collected which artefact when, with hashes; keep the collection method documented (ISO/IEC 27037 / 27043).

## 4. Retention versus minimisation
- Retention: gt24 — basis: other
- Personal data in logs: none
- [Art. 19](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/19/)(1) / [Art. 26](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/26/)(6): at least six months *unless provided otherwise in Union or national law, in particular on the protection of personal data* — the GDPR can shorten the floor, a sector term can lengthen it. Practical pattern: **layered retention** — technical metadata long, prompt/response content short, legal hold as the exception; a hash of the input proves what the input was without keeping it.

## 5. Parallel reporting clocks
One incident can start several clocks with different start triggers. Being able to *classify* within hours is what forensic readiness buys.

| Applies | Regime | Trigger | Clock starts at | Deadlines | Recipient |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Yes** | EU AI Act Art. 73 | serious incident (Art. 3(49)) | causal link established, or reasonably likely | 15 days (General serious incident) · 2 days (Widespread infringement, or a serious and irreversible disruption of the management or operation of critical infrastructure) · 10 days (Death of a person) | market surveillance authority |
| check | GDPR Art. 33 / 34 | personal-data breach | awareness | 72 h to the supervisory authority; data subjects 'without undue delay' when high risk | Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens (NL) |
| check | DORA Art. 19 | major ICT-related incident | classification (initial) / detection | initial notification 4 h after classification and no later than 24 h after detection; intermediate report 72 h; final report 1 month | financial supervisor (DNB) |
| **Yes** | NIS2 / Cyberbeveiligingswet | significant incident | awareness | 24 h early warning; 72 h notification; 1 month final report | national CSIRT / competent authority |

- _EU AI Act Art. 73_: Providers report; deployers inform the provider (Art. 26(5)) and, if the provider cannot be reached, report directly.
- _DORA Art. 19_: Deadlines per the DORA RTS/ITS on incident reporting — verify against the entity's classification thresholds.
- _NIS2 / Cyberbeveiligingswet_: Only where the organisation is an essential or important entity under the Cyberbeveiligingswet; for financial entities DORA is lex specialis.
